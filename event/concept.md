# Concept

## One sentence

An evening in Munich where two people who understand the July 2026 agent-swarm incident explain
to industry professionals, founders and students what happened, why it is evidence about
catastrophic risk from AI, and what they can do about it.

## Decided on 2026-09-25 (S15)

- Organised as **Rationality Munich** (D-5), on **Tuesday 2026-10-13** (D-4).
- **Framing (D-10):** the advertising leads with what happened. The Hugging Face incident is the
  headline, set among the other 2026 incidents, including new ones up to the event date. The
  listing and poster do not lead with existential risk; the talks do address the most serious
  risks. BlueDot's catastrophic-risk requirement is met in the content and in the application.
- The title should be a **question** the audience recognises as their own (Dave; Q17 open).
- Goal 3 below includes reaching people "who have levers of power … but who don't know what …
  is going on" (Dave, D-3).

## Framing: wider than one incident

The seed (S01) is right that marketing only "the Hugging Face incident" is too narrow and
already dated. The stronger frame, borrowed from agi.wtf's timeline card (S05), is **"the
summer AI went off script"**: nine escapes (S04), a resignation with 170M views (S08), and the
two biggest labs agreeing to slow down (S09, S12). The Hugging Face incident is the case study,
not the whole subject. The consolidated timeline is in `sources/context/timeline-summer-2026.md`.

## Working titles (pick one, or improve)

1. "What Actually Happened: the Hugging Face AI incident, explained"
2. "The Summer AI Went Off Script — a Munich briefing"
3. "700 Agents Broke Out. Now What?"
4. "Warning Shot: making sense of the 2026 AI incidents"

Subtitle in all cases: "Two short talks, open discussion, drinks. For people who deploy, build,
study or worry about AI, no background required."

## Goals

1. **Understanding**: attendees can retell the incident accurately (agents, sandbox escape,
   message board, deception, 700 attackers, nobody caught it live) and place it among the
   other 2026 escapes.
2. **Significance**: attendees see why researchers and lab CEOs treat this as a warning shot for
   loss of control, not a patchable bug. This is the BlueDot requirement that events "be focused
   on catastrophic risk" (S02).
3. **Agency**: every attendee leaves with one next step matched to who they are.
4. **Community**: the mixer connects industry people with the local AI safety, EA and PauseAI
   groups, and the organisers with each other's networks.

## Why this audience (the justification BlueDot asks for)

The seed asks us to justify targeting industry leaders rather than the already-converted (S01,
questions 1 and the conjecture paragraph). The argument:

- **They make deployment decisions.** Munich hosts the AI teams of large industrial, automotive,
  insurance and engineering firms and a dense startup scene. These are the people choosing
  whether and how to deploy autonomous agents inside real organisations in the next year. The
  Hugging Face incident is directly about what agents do when given tools, a goal and
  insufficient oversight (S03, S10).
- **They are under-served.** EA/ACX/PauseAI audiences have already read about the incident.
  Industry professionals got headlines and vendor reassurance. No local event exists that
  explains it to them at their level. BlueDot's RFE explicitly prizes "professional networks"
  and helping "newly interested people get oriented" (S02).
- **They are multipliers.** One engineering lead who understands the incident changes how a
  team of fifty thinks about agent guardrails. One founder tells their investors.
- **Students are the pipeline.** TUM students are the cheapest audience to reach and the most
  likely to follow up into BlueDot courses (S02's campus-briefing example).

We still welcome the EA/ACX/PauseAI communities: they fill the room, ask good questions, and
are the people industry attendees should meet at the mixer.

## Intended outcomes, by segment

| Segment | Understand | Do differently |
|---|---|---|
| Industry AI leads / engineers | What agents did, how ordinary weaknesses were exploited at machine speed (S10), that monitoring did not catch it (S04) | Ask the agent-deployment questions on the resource sheet before their next rollout; consider what "independent evaluation" means internally |
| Founders | That the labs themselves are calling for a slowdown and outside evaluators (S12, S09) | Factor safety posture into product and fundraising narratives; connect with local safety people |
| Students | The technical shape of the incident and the open research problems (detection, containment, S04, S13) | Join a BlueDot course or a local reading group; consider the field |
| EA / ACX / PauseAI members | The verified facts versus the rumours | Bring a colleague next time; act as connectors at the mixer |
| General public | The three-sentence version (D01) | Talk about it accurately |

## Tone

Calm, specific, no jargon, no doom theatrics, no vendor spin. agi.wtf's "secure your own
orienting before assisting others" register (S05), adapted for a professional Munich crowd.
Both talks encourage questions; long detailed talks are explicitly not wanted (S01).
