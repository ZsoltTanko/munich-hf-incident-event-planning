# Request for Events: Helping people respond to the current AI safety moment

Sep 17, 2026, [Joshua Landes](mailto:joshua@bluedot.org)& [Harry Waterman](mailto:harry@bluedot.org)

The [Hugging Face incident](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/), [Jacob Coxon's departure from Anthropic](https://x.com/hilbertspaess/status/2097476196791709843?s=20) and the broader debate about the [pace](https://x.com/sama/status/2098811563415150910) of AI development have left many people with serious questions: What actually happened? What is happening? How much should it change our expectations? What does it mean for me, my family, for the nation? How worried should we be? What can we usefully do?

BlueDot is requesting proposals for events that help people understand these developments, discuss what they mean for their communities, and consider how to respond. We will fund selected proposals through Rapid Grants.

We're especially interested in proposals from people who can reach a relevant audience and have a clear view of what bringing them together could achieve. University groups, churches and other faith communities, civic groups, neighborhood groups and professional networks are all welcome. You do not need to have organized a large event or taken a BlueDot course before, though we are more excited to fund events organized or co-organized by people with AI safety context.

**In short: If you care about reducing risks from AI, have access to people asking serious questions about recent AI developments and a plan to help them engage, we'd like to hear from you.**

# What we'd like to fund

A good proposal identifies an audience, a useful change the event could bring about, and why the event will help. We're particularly interested in events that:

* **Help newly interested people get oriented.** For example, a campus briefing on the Hugging Face incident that connects interested students with an ongoing reading group.  
* **Help people identify how they could contribute.** For example, a discussion for engineers or policy professionals that leads into a practical project or a relevant learning opportunity.  
* **Bring a local community together to make sense of AI.** For example, an event for your church community, a local civic group, or a group of neighbours or community leaders who want to understand what's happening and talk about it with people they know and trust.

We're also broadly excited about events that bring together civically engaged people and create space for thoughtful discussion. Helping a community become better informed, explore shared concerns and think together about how to respond can itself be a worthwhile outcome. Participants do not need to be considering careers in AI safety.

These are examples. **We are very open to funding many other formats with a compelling case for how they help AI go well – though we do require all of these to be focused on catastrophic risk\!** 

Organizers will have to design and run their own events. We will work to spin up an event kit to provide source links, suggested agendas, discussion prompts and follow-up templates to reduce preparation time. We’ll also host the event on our Luma, send invites to BlueDot participants in the area and help connect other AI safety people to planned events. For higher-profile events we’re also keen to help you find high AIS context speakers\!  

# How much to request

Most events should run between **$300–$4,000**. Small discussions can cost less; larger or particularly valuable events can justify up to **$10,000** and in select cases even more. 

We can consider venue hire, food & bev, access costs, materials, and clearly scoped organizer or facilitator time. For larger, more ambitious events we’d be excited to also cover speaker travel and help with speaker sourcing\! Though, the more you do around this yourself, the better\!  

For example, a 40-person event in a large US city might request $3,000 for a room, refreshments, preparation, and follow-up. Local prices and available free space will vary so please adjust accordingly. 

# How to apply

Apply through the [Rapid Grants form](https://airtable.com/appMVNtdBtvtJvu5E/pag9G3oF4DYAyassX/form). Choose **Events & community** and start your project title with **Events RFE: \[your city/group\]**.

Use the existing questions to tell us:

* Who you want to reach and how you'll invite them.  
* Your proposed date, location and agenda.  
* What you hope participants will understand or do differently.  
* Any next steps or follow-up you plan, if relevant.  
* Your estimated costs and relevant AI safety context you have.

A short draft agenda is fine for the work-link field. If you're new to BlueDot, select **Other** for your connection and explain briefly.

For this RFE, apply by **Oct 4, 2026**. We're especially interested in events that can happen by **Oct 18, 2026**; please explain if a later date would make your proposal substantially better.

We will review applications on a rolling basis. Please allow a week before you need a decision, and explain any deadline. Arrange your spending around the payment timing confirmed in your award email.

The usual [Rapid Grants eligibility, funding restrictions and terms apply](https://bluedot.org/grants/rapid).
