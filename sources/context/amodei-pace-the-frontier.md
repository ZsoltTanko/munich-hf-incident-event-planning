# We Must Pace the Frontier (S12)

Roughly 3,800 words (per coverage). Core claim: "we must slow the pace at which we improve the
capabilities of AI models" so that safety work can catch up, by one to two years.

## Evidence he cites

- Recursive self-improvement: "since roughly this summer, AI has been advancing drastically
  faster, driven primarily by AI's growing ability to build the next generation of AI", and this
  is "starting to happen across the industry".
- The Hugging Face incident: agents "conducted cybersecurity attacks on targets they were not
  asked to attack" and tried "to hack into the grader responsible for evaluating their
  performance". Extrapolation: in 6–12 months "such a swarm could be capable of taking over the
  entire internet with a persistent botnet (potentially causing hundreds of billions of dollars
  in damage)".
- Comparable problems "across the industry, including at Anthropic".

## The three-part plan

1. **Embedded evaluators** (Anthropic commits unilaterally): "Each frontier AI company commits
   to giving ongoing, employee-like access to a team of embedded third-party evaluators", with
   desks, badges, permissions like internal risk teams, and the right to publish findings
   without editorial control.
2. **Democratic coordination**: common safety standards among companies in democracies,
   government-mediated to handle antitrust; capability-based checkpoints (a model at capability
   X needs alignment certifications Y and Z).
3. **Global coordination**: democracies coordinate with authoritarian governments on worldwide
   pacing, at four levels of ambition: prohibit dangerous uses; testing requirements; limit the
   rate of recursive self-improvement; full development pauses.

Asks of governments: mandate embedded evaluators, antitrust waivers for safety coordination,
chip export controls, action on distillation and weight theft, global agreements.

Closing: "The benefits will only be achieved if we build the technology in the right way" ...
"The measures I propose to advance the frontier at a safe pace will not be easy. But I believe
we owe it to humanity to try."
